Hatcher Program

Reference SOP for hatcher management — objectives, egg shell temperature, ventilation, humidity, and hatch-window quality checks. Tap a section to expand.

1 Objectives ▾
  1. Achieving optimum egg shell temperature of 100–100.5°F.
  2. Short hatch window — ideally 24–30 hours from 1st bird to take-off.
  3. Uniformed hatch.
  4. On-time hatching (not early and not delayed).
  5. Good hatch number.
  6. Low number of culled birds.
2 Egg Shell Temperature (EST) ▾
Target EST
100–100.5°F
Monitor from
Day 1

EST is the main factor for managing incubation temperature from setter to hatcher. It affects embryo development speed, moisture loss, bird quality, and hatch percentage.

EST is the basis for adjusting the temperature program. Knowing EST from Day 1 gives an advantage in managing embryo development — the timing of adjustments depends on embryonic heat production:

  • First ~15 days: embryo relies more on outside temperature; less embryonic heat produced.
  • Later stages: embryonic heat production increases as the embryo grows — keeping the embryo cooler becomes the priority.

How to measure: keep EST monitoring consistent from Day 1 to hatching using a tiny-tag or small temperature logger placed close to the eggshell. Periodically cross-check with an ear thermometer on eggs inside the incubator.

3 Hatching — Effects of EST After Transfer ▾
EST too low: reduced hatch number, longer hatch window, wet birds, more unhatched birds, birds with large belly (omphalitis), elevated 7-day farm mortality.
EST too high: accelerated hatching, non-uniform hatch, smaller/dehydrated birds, impaired organ development. Studies show reduced heart size and impaired gut development in early brooding; more late-stage deaths from exhaustion/overheating. Hatch number drop is smaller than with low EST, but bird quality and farm performance suffer.
4 Hatcher Cabinet Temperature Profile ▾
▤Chart: New Proposed Hatcher Temperature Profile — refer to printed SOP

Step-down is timed to the percentage of birds hatched, not the clock — this avoids forcing an early or slow hatch.

Once 100% of birds have hatched, the machine becomes a holding cabinet and must be turned down significantly to prevent overheating.

Comfort cloacal temp
104–105°F
Use for
Step-down timing

Measuring cloacal temperature at this stage tells you the birds' actual comfort level and guides the step-down before take-off.

5 Ventilation ▾

Poor ventilation effects are hard to see at the hatchery but show up on the farm: high CO₂ leads to flat, lethargic birds with low 7-day bodyweights and elevated 7-day mortality.

Controlled elevated CO₂ can still help hatching — it narrows the hatch window and encourages a good transition from embryonic respiration to lung ventilation. Keeping CO₂ high until pipping can be beneficial.

Hard limit: keep CO₂ under 3000 ppm (or less) once birds are out, to prevent embryonic death and support post-hatch development.
6 Humidity ▾

Humidity softens shell membranes so birds can emerge easily.

Set point after transfer
60% RH
Peak during pipping
>80% RH

RH naturally rises above 80% during pipping/hatching from moisture release, then should gradually decrease back to just above 60% once all birds are out.

Too high: prolongs hatching, affects hatch quality and window.
Too low: also hurts both quality and hatch number.

Once birds are all out, keep humidity controlled — it directly affects heat index inside the hatcher.

8 Hatch Window — What to Check ▾
▤Reference: Ideal hatch spread photo — refer to printed SOP

Hatch spread should look closely similar to the reference. Also assess these physical attributes for hatch timing:

  • Damp feathers: expect slightly damp feathers at the back/neck on a few ducklings (~5% at take-off).
  • Hocks/feet: should not be dry or skinny. Protruding veins around the hock/feet indicate the bird stayed in the hatcher too long and is possibly dehydrated.
  • Meconium level: too much poop in the eggs indicates birds stayed longer in the hatcher and started secreting to cool themselves.
  • Hatch quality check: perform during grading and record the hatch score.
